# STRAT 400 — Strategies for Sustainable Development
Winter 2026 | MW 1:00–2:30pm | 26 sessions | Prof. Andrew Hoffman | TA: Ella Kaplan | Ross School of Business, University of Michigan

## Course Overview
Covers the basic concepts by which corporations address sustainability issues and prepares students for cutting-edge issues on the horizon. Foundational topics: **triple bottom line, sustainability reporting, impact investing, the LOHAS consumer, the UN Sustainable Development Goals, sustainable operations and competitive strategy.** Cutting-edge topics: circular economy, sharing economy, human rights, conflict minerals, alternative economies, sustainable consumption.

Sustainability is reframed as a **market shift** — pressures from market constituents (governments, consumers, investors, insurance companies) translate sustainability into familiar business frameworks: risk management (insurance pressure), strategic direction and market growth (competitor pressure), capital acquisition (investor pressure). The firm's business channels bring environmental/social issues to managerial attention through marketing, accounting, finance, product development, etc.

Two parts: **Part 1 "Enterprise Integration"** (sustainability as a mainstream business issue within existing business parameters) and **Part 2 "Market Transformation"** (how those parameters must shift).

## Learning / Assignments
- **Position Papers (2) — 20%:** op-ed-style argued stands on sustainability tradeoffs (WSJ/NYT op-ed framing); grounded in models, evidence, analysis of tradeoffs.
- **Class Citizenship — 20%:** applying theory/concepts correctly; reasoned analysis.
- **Reflective Journals (2) — 20%:** key takeaways + a visual "mental model" integrating concepts into career impact, grounded in real market signals.
- **Sustainability Case Study (Group) — 40%:** write an 8-page business case + 4-page teaching note about a company facing a market-transformation challenge — central question, protagonist who must make a decision, with financial, technical, marketing, political, PR, and strategic considerations. Best cases become real teaching cases used in future classes.

## Key Topics / Cases (relevance to ES 212)
- **Triple bottom line; business sustainability as a market shift** (Sessions 1–2) — *directly matches ES 212 #6's Triple Bottom Line reading*
- **Entering the green market; business models** — Molten Metal Technology case analyzes "the overall business model" and commercialization risks for a high-tech startup
- **Market Drivers / the LOHAS consumer; consumer behavior, green marketing** (Session 4) — Clorox GreenWorks: market entry, first-mover vs. fast-follower strategy, brand/market share
- **Social Drivers; ESG disclosure, impact investing** (Sessions 5–6) — investor perspective, capital allocation
- **International Drivers; supply chains, externalities** (Session 7) — tragedy of the commons, comparative advantage / free trade economics, H&M global supply chain
- **Opening New Markets** (Session 8) — real estate, climate risk, LEED; market opportunity from a developer's perspective; value proposition
- **Insurance and Science; risk management** (Session 10) — business case analysis, actuarial/risk pricing, internalizing externalities
- **The Future of Mobility** (Session 11) — Ford "Blueprint for Mobility": business model transformation, value proposition in a new market, partnerships, market positioning
- **Reexamining Capitalism; market incentives (cap and trade / carbon pricing)** (Session 15)
- **Corporate Leadership; UN SDGs, human rights, supply chain transparency** (Session 16) — Intel conflict minerals: competitive advantage analysis
- **The Future of Food** (Session 17) — JUST Inc.: market opportunity selection, value propositions, first-mover entry strategy, go-to-market next steps
- **Pre-Competitive Collaboration** (Session 19) — competition vs. collaboration, antitrust, market mechanisms
- **Innovative Corporate Forms** (Session 20) — Benefit Corporations, B Corp, L3C / low-profit LLC governance structures; scaling a venture; Schumpeter's creative destruction — *relevant to ES 212 #10 company formation/structure*
- **Promoting Local Economies** (Session 22) — BerkShares local currency: import-replacement, productive loans, entrepreneurship, grants/philanthropy funding
- **Carbon Neutral/Negative** (Session 23) — Interface: competitive landscape shaping sustainable innovation
- **Investment and Divestment** (Session 24) — endowment investing, fiduciary duty vs. social responsibility, capital allocation
- **Questioning Consumerism** (Session 25) — Patagonia: marketing power, consumption, brand strategy
