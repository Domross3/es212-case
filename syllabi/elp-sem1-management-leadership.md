# ENTR 490.013 — Entrepreneurial Leadership Program (ELP), Semester 1: Management and Leadership
Winter 2025 | 3 credit hours | Tue/Thu 1:30–3:00 PM | Center for Entrepreneurship (CFE), College of Engineering, University of Michigan
Faculty: Grace Hsia Haberl, Ms.E; Mitchell Rohde, Ph.D. | Program: ELP (competitive, application-required cohort)

## Course Overview
CFE ENTR courses develop an entrepreneurial mindset across six behaviors: opportunity identification, innovation, experimentation, relationship building, risk management, and perseverance. ENTR 490.013 immerses students in the entrepreneurial process. This Management and Leadership course prepares ELP students for a successful startup or entrepreneurial experience by developing the leadership, operations, innovation, and execution skills critical to being entrepreneurial leaders.

Format is "show, discuss, do" — lectures, interactive discussion, then reinforcing activities. Many sessions feature guest experts (experienced leaders and entrepreneurs). **This is a rigorous course requiring competitive application for admittance into the ELP Cohort; held to expectations higher than standard upperclassman and graduate-level courses.**

## Primary Course Topic Categories
- **Individual Leadership:** Communication, Networking, Etiquette
- **Operations:** Business Models, Legal Overview, Finance and Investment
- **Innovation and Execution:** Brainstorming, Creativity, Innovation, Project and Program Management, Sales, and Marketing

## Assessable Learning Outcomes
By the end of the course a successful learner will be able to:
- **Understand and Practice Leadership:** lead teams and ventures
- **Understand startups:** through case studies and key principles
- **Conduct Industry Assessments:** deeper understanding of specific industries via research and analysis
- **Perform Opportunity Identification:** identify opportunities for innovation
- **Complete Validation:** actively test approaches, ideas, products, and services to develop opportunities

## Grading / Deliverables
- Attendance and Participation — 15%
- Group Projects — 35%
- Individual Exercises and Assignments — 35%
- Coaching Connection Program — 15% (monthly entrepreneurial coaching sessions)

**Group Projects** apply project management, marketing, finance, legal, and sales from a startup perspective, including: running customer discovery experiments, building prototypes, creating an e-commerce portal, creating and launching marketing material, consulting with an attorney for a contract or legal document, and making sales.

## Course Textbooks (signal coverage of specific ES 212 topics)
- **The Startup Owner's Manual** — Bob Dorf & Steve Blank (customer discovery, customer development)
- **Business Model Generation** — Osterwalder & Pigneur (business models / Business Model Canvas)
- **Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than Your Lawyer and Venture Capitalist** — Feld & Mendelson (startup financing, term sheets, investor/legal)
- **The Essentials of Finance and Accounting for Nonfinancial Managers** — Fields (accounting & financial statements)
- **Financial Literacy for Managers: Finance and Accounting for Better Decision-Making** — Lambert (finance & accounting for decisions)
- **Financial and Managerial Accounting for MBAs** — Easton et al. (financial & managerial accounting)

## Skills developed (CoE project guidelines)
Written reports and oral presentations to a broad audience; problem-solving and creative design process; teamwork and team management; decision-making; professional responsibility; societal impact and sustainability.

## Workload
For every 1 contact hour, 2–3 hours of curricular engagement expected. 14-week semester.
