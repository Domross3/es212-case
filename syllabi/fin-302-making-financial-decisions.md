# FIN 302 — Making Financial Decisions
Fall 2024 | MW (two sections) | Instructor: Shane H. Miller | Ross School of Business, University of Michigan

## Course Description and Objectives
Financial analysis provides a structure to estimate and compare the costs and benefits of decisions made in personal life and within an organization/firm. Students develop a foundational understanding of decision-making using financial analysis and the ability to apply that framework to make personal investing decisions, **allocate capital within an organization or firm, and determine the value of a firm or any asset that produces cash flows.** Students also learn to **assess risk, understand the relationship between risk and return** when investing, and gain a general understanding of financial markets. Extensive examples, problem solving, and case studies.

## Textbook
*Fundamentals of Corporate Finance*, 6th Ed., Berk, DeMarzo, and Harford (BDH).

## Topics / Schedule
**Module 1: Core Valuation Tools**
1. Introduction to Finance
2. Present Value of a Single Cash Flow
3. Present Value of Multiple Cash Flows (time value of money)

**Module 2: Valuing Financial Contracts, Projects, and Firms**
4. Interest Rates and Bond Valuation
5. **A Reintroduction to Accounting for Financial Analysis** (financial statements)
6. **Stock and Firm Valuation**
7. **Project Valuation and Investment Decision Rules** (NPV, IRR — capital budgeting)

**Module 3: Discount Rates**
8. Probability and Statistics for Risk and Return
9. Portfolio Theory
10. Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
11. Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

**Module 4: Capital Structure, Financing Decisions, and Derivatives**
12. Market Efficiency
13. **Capital Structure** (financing decisions — debt vs. equity)
14. Basics of Derivatives

## Relevance to ES 212 mapping
- **Startup projections and financials (#12) — STRONG, NAMED COURSE.** ES 212 lists "intro to financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement); assumptions for financial projections; value-creating milestones." FIN 302 provides the rigorous foundation: accounting for financial analysis, present value / cash-flow projection, stock and firm valuation, project valuation. This replaces the textbook-inferred coverage (ELP finance texts) with a graded Ross core finance course.
- **Funding / investor perspective / startup financing (#13):** firm and stock valuation, what determines value, risk and return, cost of capital (what investors require), capital structure (debt vs. equity financing decisions) — the analytical core of how investors value a venture.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty (ES 212 skills objective):** risk/return analysis, probability and statistics for investment decisions, investment decision rules.
- **Business viability analysis (ES 212 skills objective):** project valuation and investment decision rules (NPV/IRR) — the quantitative basis for assessing venture viability.
