# ENTR 490.015 — Entrepreneurial Leadership Program (ELP), Semester 2: Entrepreneurial Action and Vision
Fall 2025 | 3 credit hours | Tue/Thu 1:30–3:00 PM | Center for Entrepreneurship (CFE), College of Engineering, University of Michigan
Faculty: Grace Hsia Haberl, Ms.E; Mitchell Rohde, Ph.D. | Program: ELP (competitive, application-required cohort)

## Course Overview
The Entrepreneurial Leadership Program provides deep training and mentorship for a select cohort of students to develop the functional, managerial, and leadership skills that differentiate the good from the great entrepreneurs. It equips some of the University of Michigan's most driven minds with a rigorous experiential curriculum to lead them onto an entrepreneurial path during and after their college career.

Entrepreneurial Action and Vision is the second ELP course, building on Semester 1. In this advanced practicum, students will:
- Learn and apply fundamental entrepreneurship principles, with a particular focus on **leadership, team building, and culture**
- Learn and apply **entrepreneurship strategy skills around early decision making, marketing, and relations**
- Learn and apply **project management skills** through a capstone project
- Analyze and evaluate personal growth with entrepreneurial roles and goals

## Two Primary Elements

### 1. Executive Entrepreneurial Topic focus
Learning modules on project management, strategy skills and decision making, and building a team. Individual assignments assess knowledge and growth; individual reflections required.

### 2. Capstone Project (~2/3 of course effort)
**Format: NSF SBIR Phase I Project Pitch.** A major initiative of any startup is early resourcefulness and capital for the development of MVPs and operational runway to mitigate risk in advance of potential investment. Accessing government grants, business model competitions, and other early sources of funding is an important skill of any entrepreneurial leader. Students work in teams to pursue an entrepreneurial project and formulate goals, objectives, and outcomes as if it were a Project Pitch to the NSF for Phase I funding. All students submit a capstone proposal and pitch; 5–6 projects are selected for advancement based on idea, feasibility, impact, team, and stage.

## Assessable Learning Outcomes
By the end of this course, a successful ELP learner will be able to:
- **Describe, Discuss, Explain:** fundamental entrepreneurship principles — leadership, team building, culture. Utilize frameworks for setting SMART goals related to team-building and company founding.
- **Create, Design, Manage:** entrepreneurship strategy skills around early decision making, marketing, and relations. Manage projects using tools such as GANTT charts. **Conceptualize and write Project Pitch applications for early-stage company formation and funding, with specific emphasis on writing Technical Objectives.**
- **Apply, Construct, Demonstrate:** project management skills via capstone. **Develop early-stage plans for the commercialization of a technology using customer discovery methods.**
- **Analyze, Experiment, Test:** **Apply the business model concepts of the first semester course to formulate a go-to-market strategy** for a capstone project. Grow a startup team.
- **Evaluate, Interpret, Assess:** Analyze and evaluate personal growth with entrepreneurial roles and goals.
