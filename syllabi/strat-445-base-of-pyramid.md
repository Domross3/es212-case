# BA/STRAT 445 — Base of the Pyramid: Business Innovation and Social Impact
Fall 2024 — Section 002 | Prof. Ted London (Ford Motor Company Clinical Professor of Business Administration) | TA: Sid Makkar | Ross School of Business, University of Michigan

## Course Overview
Prospects for a more inclusive capitalism are appearing on the horizon. While poverty, inequity and exclusion endure despite the globalization of business and substantial investments by the development community, two emerging trends offer hope for a better future.

Facing saturated markets and highly competitive landscapes, profit-seeking firms and entrepreneurial enterprises familiar with serving the wealthy are broadening their search for new markets. One outcome is that companies are increasingly viewing the base of the pyramid (BoP) — the 4-5 billion people living in low-income markets in the developing world — as an enormous and untapped market opportunity.

Concurrently, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which are meant to guide investments by the development community, view profit-making enterprises serving the BoP as an important and underutilized tool for alleviating poverty. For these BoP enterprises to achieve their potential, business and development partners must find ways to productively engage, while overcoming substantial differences in mindsets, metrics and missions.

BoP enterprises encounter difficulties in developing successful business models in an unfamiliar and impoverished market context. Sustainability at scale requires new perspectives on value creation and capabilities that respect and leverage the skills and resources of the BoP.

## Course Learning Objectives
In taking this course, students will:
1. Develop a rich understanding of the unique opportunities and challenges associated with doing business with the BoP;
2. Assess the performance of BoP enterprises seeking to deliver financial returns and social impact by leveraging a toolkit of strategies, frameworks and processes; and
3. Formulate actionable recommendations and strategies for building sustainable, scalable BoP enterprises that also materially improve the well-being of the most impoverished individuals and communities on the planet.

## Action-Based Learning
Action-based learning is a central feature of the class, embedded in both the group project and the interactive lab sessions held with visiting BoP enterprise leaders.

**Group Project:** Students advise a venture's board of directors — reviewing the enterprise, assessing current status, and providing recommendations for enhancements. Requires critical analysis (not just description), with actionable recommendations specifying what should be done, by whom and when. Deliverables spread across the semester: initial venture synopsis (written + presentation), mid-semester business-model presentation, final presentation, and final report.

**Interactive Labs:** Two 3-hour working sessions with visiting BoP enterprise leaders. Teams prepare and submit implementable recommendations to real-time challenges faced by the guests' organizations, then discuss and refine in class.

## Course Deliverables (Grade Components)
- **Class Contribution — 30%**: Thoughtful contribution to class discussion; post-class discussion forum; missed-class video responses.
- **Pre-Class Discussion Questions — 6.67% each x 3**: Analyze enterprise using relevant framework, identify key takeaways, propose implementable recommendations. ~½–1 page each.
- **Interactive Lab Assessments (Team) — 7.5% each x 2**: ~1–3 pages per question addressing guests' real-time challenges.
- **Group Project: Venture Assessment — 35%**: Venture synopsis (1 page + 60-sec presentation), venture update (5-min presentation, 3-4 slides), final presentation + Q&A, ~25-page final report.
- **Final Exam: None**
