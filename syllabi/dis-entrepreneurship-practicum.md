# Entrepreneurship Practicum (Study Abroad Internship)
Summer 2024 | DIS Copenhagen (Denmark) | Internship — 3 credits | Faculty: Kristian B. Knudsen (CFA, MSc Finance/Management; owner/MD of a business consulting firm + private equity company)
Corequisite: Innovation and Entrepreneurship in Europe
Major Disciplines: Business, Entrepreneurship, Consulting

> NOTE: Dom's entrepreneurship internship — embedded ~100 hours inside a real Danish startup, paired with the DIS incubator course.

## Description
Students work closely with a Danish startup and experience first-hand the challenges young companies face getting products and services to market. Focuses on the specific challenges of the assigned startup and its competitors, plus the student's personal journey into the entrepreneurial lifestyle and building a network in Denmark.

Contrasts the corporate model (efficiencies and execution) with the entrepreneurial model (**business model search, constant iteration, "managed chaos"**), which requires resilience and networking skills.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of the internship you will:
- Be familiar with the practical challenges of working in a startup setting
- Have a balanced view of pros/cons of startup vs. corporate environments
- Understand the structure of the space your startup operates in, its **competitive landscape**, and changing dynamics
- Know how to add value to a startup by leveraging academic strengths, personality, and network
- Have experience working in a startup with fluid job descriptions, changing priorities, flat hierarchies, high flexibility
- Have experience managing time and expectations, negotiating trade-offs, and **prioritizing tasks** in an ever-changing environment
- Take on multiple roles and manage a fluid job description
- Reflect on your personal abilities and self-efficacy as an entrepreneur

## Approach
Run like a professional consulting company approaches project management. Students work on specific assignments with the designated startup, solved and managed in a structured, dedicated way. ~20 hours/week at the startup (~100 hours total), plus reflection and feedback sessions. Students must be proactive — determine their own tasks, set expectations, and define tangible output.

## Grading
- Various blog discussions — 20%
- **Project management, proactiveness and commitment — 40%**
- **Performance assessment by the company — 30%** (real startup evaluates the student's contribution)
- Final report — 10%

## Schedule
1. Introduction and startup assignment
2. Startup bootcamp (presentation and helping the team)
3. Update on practicum progress (define practicum project, process/timeline, solution to business problem)
4. Concluding and reflecting
- Weekly project updates (time + status/progress log every Friday)

## Relevance to ES 212 mapping
- **What entrepreneurship is / key challenges (#1):** direct, lived exposure to startup realities — the practical challenges, the lifestyle, the difference from corporate work.
- **Project management & working on teams (ES 212 skills objectives):** the core graded competency — managing a real project for a real company, proactiveness, prioritization, time management under uncertainty.
- **Entrepreneurial teams / roles (#11):** fluid job descriptions, multiple roles, flat hierarchies, forging a useful place on a small team.
- **Market / competitive landscape (#9, supporting):** understanding the startup's space, competitors, and changing dynamics.
- **Business viability / solving real business problems (ES 212 skills, #5):** "solution to business problem" deliverable; adding value to a live venture.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty (ES 212 skills):** negotiating trade-offs and prioritizing in an ever-changing environment.
- UNIQUE: real-world performance evaluated by an actual company — evidence of applied entrepreneurial capability beyond the classroom.
