# ES 212 / UC 214 — Entrepreneurial Business Basics (THE COURSE TO WAIVE)
Fall 2025 | Section 003 | Tue/Thu 10:00–11:30 AM, Room B1580 (Blau) | Instructor: Mike Johnson, MD (mljohn@umich.edu) | University of Michigan

## Course Overview
This course introduces business basics that are useful in entrepreneurial businesses and across business stages. It introduces foundational business concepts and considers how entrepreneurial ventures create value — what makes for a good entrepreneurial idea, how entrepreneurial ventures work, and how entrepreneurial companies turn ideas into products and services. Students evaluate opportunities and challenges in entrepreneurial businesses, understand ways to mitigate risks, and develop a foundation to further understand entrepreneurship and business.

Designed for undergraduates from many disciplines with differing levels of business experience. **Note: not open to students pursuing a BBA or other Ross program. No prerequisites.**

## Learning Objectives
Introduces fundamental concepts in business and entrepreneurship and essential skill development: innovation/creativity, analytical and integrative thinking, decision-making under uncertainty, project management and working on diverse teams, and communicating effectively.

### Knowledge objectives
- Foundational business areas, concepts, and terminology
- What entrepreneurship is and why it matters to society
- Key challenges faced by entrepreneurs
- How to understand customer needs
- How entrepreneurs create value for customers and users
- Different types of business models
- Product design and development
- Markets and market research
- Introductory economic ideas
- Introductory accounting concepts and startup financials
- Company formation and team-building
- Startup financing and investor perspective
- Startup presentations and plans

### Skills objectives
- Develop hypotheses on entrepreneurial value creation
- Gather relevant, important information and effectively synthesize findings from research and data
- Communicate clearly and effectively through presentations and written deliverables
- Develop project management and team skills
- Develop business viability analysis skills
- Formulate an innovation strategy and apply design skills through action-based learning

## Grading
- Class contribution (individual) — 10%
- Quizzes (individual) — 30% (7 quizzes, lowest dropped, best 6 count)
- Final exam (individual, in-person) — 20%

### Project
Develop a proposal for a new venture, working as individuals and as an entrepreneurial team. Includes: startup needs assignment (individual), hypothesis on startup value creation (individual), business model drafting (team), in-class elevator pitch (team), in-class final presentation with slide deck and memo on risks/mitigation/venture viability (team).

## Course Modules and Topics (THE SYLLABUS UNITS TO MAP AGAINST)

1. **Introduction to business, entrepreneurship, and this course**
   - Foundational business concepts and terminology
   - What is entrepreneurship? Different types of entrepreneurship
   - Key tasks and challenges in entrepreneurship

2. **Entrepreneurship and creating value**
   - Thinking about entrepreneurial problems and customer needs
   - What is a value proposition?

3. **Trends, technology, and thinking about the future**
   - Trends analysis and technology forecasting
   - Entrepreneurial opportunities in trends
   - Reading: "How Computers Changed Everything", Johnson

4. **Intro to business models**
   - What is a business model? Different business model concepts
   - How a business model is developed and used
   - Reading: "Business Model Analysis for Entrepreneurs", Eisenmann

5. **Entrepreneurial opportunities and business models**
   - What makes an entrepreneurial opportunity worth pursuing?
   - Intro to presentations and plans
   - Reading: "Hypothesis-Driven Entrepreneurship", Eisenmann, Ries, Dillard

6. **Entrepreneurship and society**
   - How social entrepreneurship ventures create value
   - Needs, opportunities, and measuring impact
   - Reading: University of Michigan Social Venture Fund on Triple Bottom Line

7. **Basic economic ideas for startups**
   - Markets and pricing; supply and demand
   - Pricing and elasticity, substitutes and complements
   - Reading: "Marketing Analysis Toolkit: Pricing and Profitability Analysis", Steenburgh, Avery

8. **Design and production of products and services**
   - What is customer discovery?
   - Product design, product development phases
   - Customer feedback, iterative process
   - "Customer Movie Experience" exercise

9. **Market entry planning, marketing, and selling, product/market fit**
   - Describing and sizing a market, market research techniques
   - Promotion and selling in a startup
   - Product/market fit and startup metrics
   - Risks and mitigation
   - Readings: "Food Truck Challenge", Roberto; "How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product/Market Fit", Vohra

10. **Forming a company**
    - Choosing a business structure
    - Introduction to business contracts and intellectual property (IP)
    - Regulatory context for entrepreneurial ventures

11. **Entrepreneurial teams**
    - Building an entrepreneurial team
    - Establishing roles and founder shares
    - Readings: "Lean in Stories: Heather Hiles"; "The Management Team: While Building Usage", Wilson

12. **Startup projections and financials**
    - Assumptions for financial projections
    - Intro to financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement)
    - Value-creating milestones
    - Reading: Notes on Projections and Milestones, Johnson

13. **Funding for Startups**
    - Overview of funding sources
    - What do investors look for?
    - Plans and presentations
    - Reading: "Financing New Ventures", Kerr, Nanda

14. **Fitting it all together**
    - Synthesis / takeaways
